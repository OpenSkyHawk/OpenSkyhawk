    Power Distribution Unit (PDU)

    Each console gets one PDU: the board where that console's power comes in. It takes 12 V and 5 V from the supply, passes each rail through its own fuse, and feeds the console's bus — and it measures everything it feeds. Voltage and current on both rails, plus the board's temperature, go out over CAN to the host app about once a second, and at once when something goes wrong.
    Role Per-console power injection, fusing and monitoring — a CAN node like any other
    Node IDs 3 (Left), 4 (Right), 5 (Center)
    Microcontroller STM32F103 — same core, CAN transceiver and status LED as the base boards
    Measures 12 V and 5 V rail voltage and current, board temperature
    Fuses 12 V 5 A, 5 V 2 A — standard MINI blade
    Revision 0.2.0

    What it does

    • Fuses each rail at the injection point — a fault in one console opens only that console's fuse. Fuses are swappable automotive MINI blades, so a rating can change without touching the board.
    • Measures what it feeds — rail voltage (average and lowest dip), current (average and peak), and board temperature, sampled a thousand times a second. Logging a console through idle, startup, full lighting and its peaks shows its real load, so its fuses can be sized from data.
    • Raises the alarm — a rail sagging below or rising above its limits, a current near the fuse rating, a blown 12 V fuse, or the board running hot. A fault turns the node amber in the host app's node list and is reported straight away, not on the next poll.
    • Shows its rails without the microcontroller — three green power LEDs, one each for 12 V, 5 V and 3.3 V, are wired straight to their rails and light whenever that rail is present, even if the firmware isn't running.

    What the LEDs do and don't show

    Rail faults are reported to the host app only. The red/green status LEDs work exactly as on every other board — CAN link and bus health (see Debugging) — and don't change for a rail fault. The green power LEDs only show that a rail is present: one goes out on a dead rail or a blown fuse, but all stay lit through an over-voltage, over-current or overheat.

    Where it sits

    PSU ──► PDU (fuse + measure) ──► console bus ──► that console's boards

    Ground and CAN run continuously through every PDU; only 12 V and 5 V are split per console.

    Power never chains past a PDU

    A console's downstream harness must not feed another PDU's console. A blown fuse then leaves that console's rail dead — which is exactly how the PDU detects it. See the wiring assumptions on Power.

    Power-in pinout — crimp the harness to the board

    The 4-pin Mini-Fit Jr power input is pin 1 = +12 V · pin 2 = +5 V · pin 3 = GND · pin 4 = GND. +12 V and +5 V sit side by side — check the harness against this before first power-up.

    A blown 5 V fuse powers the PDU itself down, so the host app shows that node going offline rather than a fuse alert. The Gateway/Bridge head node also takes its power from one of the PDUs — if the whole cockpit drops off at once, check the PDU fuses first.

    Bring-up and calibration

    Each PDU is calibrated once, at bring-up. The values are stored on the board and survive firmware updates.

    1. Flash the PDU for its console, e.g. pio run -d Firmware/Panels/PDU_Left -t upload (PDU_Right / PDU_Center for the others — the code is identical, only the node ID differs).
    2. Open the debug serial console at 115200 baud. The PDU prints its node ID and calibration state at boot; CAL SHOW prints it again.
    3. Supply reference: measure the board's 3.3 V rail with a multimeter and enter it, in millivolts — CAL VDDA 3297. Until this is done the PDU assumes exactly 3.3 V.
    4. Current zero: disconnect the console load, then run CAL ZERO. The PDU refuses if it still sees current flowing. The 5 V reading includes the PDU's own small draw, so afterwards it shows the console's load only.

    RAW prints the live readings at any time, and CAL CLEAR returns the board to uncalibrated.

    Thresholds are starting points

    The limits — under / over-voltage, over-current (90 % of each fuse), temperature — are defaults to tune against your console's measured load. Each can be changed per build without a code change; the list is on Build Flags.
